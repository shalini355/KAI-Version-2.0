# AI-Tell Audit

Read-only audit of the current project, grouped by the requested categories. This records concrete instances and recurring patterns for a later polish pass; no application files have been changed. The audit covers the checked-in app, source, configuration, and documentation, not dependency contents in `bun.lock`.

## UI Copy

- [LandingPage.tsx](src/pages/LandingPage.tsx) leans on polished wellness-marketing language and repeated reassurance: “Everything you need to exhale,” “mindful sanctuary,” “tender conversational check-ins,” “find your calm,” “grounding somatic support,” and “Whenever you're ready, we're here.” The opening also claims “40k+ late-night deep breaths taken,” “100% private, no tracking,” and “client encrypted” without evidence in the app. Its FAQ claims client encryption, no model training, no sale of behavioral data, and a forever-free service; these are absolute product promises rather than supported, scoped statements.
- [ChatPage.tsx](src/pages/ChatPage.tsx) repeats “safe sanctuary,” “no-judgment midnight sanctuary,” “client encrypted,” and “100% private & client-side encrypted.” The fallback/demo exchanges in [chatService.ts](src/services/chatService.ts) sound uniformly composed and metaphor-heavy (“50-tabs feeling,” “nervous system is working in overdrive,” “profound micro-gift,” “step by quiet step”). Its no-key server fallback in [server.ts](server.ts) uses the same soothing cadence and offers a breathing exercise regardless of what the person wrote.
- [DashboardPage.tsx](src/pages/DashboardPage.tsx) repeats “gently,” “safe spaces,” “soft & grounded,” “personal vault,” and “drop the luggage.” “Mostly calm with a spike of Thursday study stress,” “Last spoke 3 hrs ago,” and fixed reflection timestamps read like scripted dashboard filler rather than values derived from the current user.
- [OnboardingPage.tsx](src/pages/OnboardingPage.tsx) uses “Setting Up Your Safe Space,” “Kai seamlessly understands,” “hold gentle space,” “Soft digital tap,” and “Enter Sanctuary.” The “Late Night Sleep spirals” capitalization and “Mai yaha hu” sample also need a natural-language proofread.
- [LoginPage.tsx](src/pages/LoginPage.tsx) and [SignupPage.tsx](src/pages/SignupPage.tsx) repeat “safe space,” “sanctuary,” “gently,” and broad reassurances such as “completely private.” The sign-up screen says “Join Kai Sanctuary”; loading labels say “Opening sanctuary...” and “Creating sanctuary...”. Generic fallback errors (“Failed to log in.” / “Signup failed.”) are abrupt rather than helpful. The login/signup screens also promise privacy without explaining the actual storage and server behavior.
- [WellnessPage.tsx](src/pages/WellnessPage.tsx) uses the same calming vocabulary throughout (“Sensory tools & grounding sanctuary,” “Daily Whisper Prompt,” “Gentle Reflection,” “Saved to your private scratchpad,” “Updated your favorite anchors”). The few demo affirmations all have the same polished, earnest cadence.
- [SupportPage.tsx](src/pages/SupportPage.tsx) calls its counselor directory “verified,” “certified,” and “licensed,” and describes the entries as available for direct contact even though the records are demonstration content. “Non-pathologizing, evidence-based” and the psychoeducation copy make clinical/evidence claims without sources. [Footer.tsx](src/components/Footer.tsx) says “Private, anonymous & strictly encrypted,” another unqualified promise.
- [SettingsPage.tsx](src/pages/SettingsPage.tsx) uses grand product language including “Full sovereign control,” “Anonymous Guest Shield,” and “ultra-calm sanctuary.” [ErrorBoundary.tsx](src/components/ErrorBoundary.tsx) and [NotFoundPage.tsx](src/pages/NotFoundPage.tsx) turn ordinary recovery states into repeated “safe space,” “sanctuary,” and “gentle breath” language. [OfflineBanner.tsx](src/components/OfflineBanner.tsx) promises reflections and logs will be “safely preserved” without explaining browser/device limits.
- Across the UI, the same sentence shape appears often: reassurance, a soft metaphor, then an invitation to breathe or continue. Emojis are present in headings, toasts, statuses, chips, and buttons in addition to the mood-sticker use where they carry meaning. The copy frequently uses em dashes and overuses words such as “gentle,” “safe,” “sanctuary,” “soft,” “tender,” and “vault.”
- Privacy wording is especially misleading: chat messages are POSTed to the app server and then Gemini in [server.ts](server.ts), so “client-side encrypted,” “end-to-end encrypted,” and “chats are stored on-device” do not describe the full chat path. The application also has no real encryption implementation. Review these statements before polishing their tone; they are product accuracy issues, not just voice.

## Visual Design

- Repeated dashboard and feature tiles follow one template: rounded surface card, colored icon in a circle, title, supporting copy, and a pill-shaped action. This pattern appears in [LandingPage.tsx](src/pages/LandingPage.tsx), [DashboardPage.tsx](src/pages/DashboardPage.tsx), [WellnessPage.tsx](src/pages/WellnessPage.tsx), and [SupportPage.tsx](src/pages/SupportPage.tsx). The landing feature grid and dashboard bento repeat similarly sized cards and matching spacing, so the visual hierarchy feels generated from one component recipe rather than varied by content.
- Gradients, large blurred color fields, pulsing dots/orbs, glass blur, and soft shadows recur across the landing hero and feature cards, dashboard cards, chat header, shared background, navbar, and orb. See [BackgroundAura.tsx](src/components/BackgroundAura.tsx), [LandingPage.tsx](src/pages/LandingPage.tsx), [DashboardPage.tsx](src/pages/DashboardPage.tsx), [ChatPage.tsx](src/pages/ChatPage.tsx), [Navbar.tsx](src/components/Navbar.tsx), and [KaiOrb.tsx](src/components/KaiOrb.tsx). Most are decorative repetitions rather than cues that communicate state.
- The palette is consistently cool teal/blue with a purple secondary and frequent pale gradient blends in [index.css](src/index.css). It is coherent, but the whole screen family has little material or tonal variation beyond those same accents.
- Typography loads Inter and Plus Jakarta Sans from Google Fonts in [index.html](index.html); both are used broadly as generic sans-serif UI/display choices. The same font pair is also declared in [index.css](src/index.css).
- [KaiOrb.tsx](src/components/KaiOrb.tsx) and [index.html](index.html) use a long external Googleusercontent image URL for the orb and favicon. The identity therefore depends on a remote generated-looking asset instead of a local, deliberate Kai mark; the fallback is another generic gradient orb. This also creates an external-asset availability dependency.
- The UI primarily uses Material Symbols rather than the requested mix of illustrated/custom visual details. There is no custom hand-drawn heading detail, background texture, or student-team attribution in [Footer.tsx](src/components/Footer.tsx).
- Layouts use responsive Tailwind breakpoints in many places, but there are no checked-in viewport screenshots or UI tests establishing behavior at 375px, 768px, and 1440px. That makes the requested size-specific polish an unverified area rather than a confirmed defect.

## Code Style

- Several page components are very large and combine unrelated view states and behavior: [ChatPage.tsx](src/pages/ChatPage.tsx), [WellnessPage.tsx](src/pages/WellnessPage.tsx), [DashboardPage.tsx](src/pages/DashboardPage.tsx), and [LandingPage.tsx](src/pages/LandingPage.tsx). Chat, dashboard, and wellness each contain extensive UI plus timers, audio, forms, or data shaping; [SupportPage.tsx](src/pages/SupportPage.tsx) also combines directory data, filtering, modal handling, and educational content.
- Banner/section comments narrate nearby JSX or repeat identifiers rather than explaining a non-obvious decision. Examples include route/layout comments in [App.tsx](src/App.tsx), large `Bento`/`TAB`/`Step` comment labels in page components, and “Load Sessions,” “Filtered Sessions,” “Compute password strength,” and “Return JSX”-style annotations across pages and services. Some comments do explain implementation choices, such as audio-noise generation, and should be retained where they explain why.
- Generic names recur: `handleSubmit` in both auth pages; `item` in mood seed generation; `result` in chart data shaping; `data` in export logic; and short callback names such as `s`, `m`, and `c` across service/page maps. Several action names are broad `handle...` wrappers where the domain action could be clearer.
- `any` is used for caught errors, browser audio/speech APIs, route state, parsed local-storage values, and type casts (notably in [server.ts](server.ts), [ChatPage.tsx](src/pages/ChatPage.tsx), [WellnessPage.tsx](src/pages/WellnessPage.tsx), and [LandingPage.tsx](src/pages/LandingPage.tsx)). Empty catches also silently swallow audio initialization/cleanup failures in page components.
- Similar local-storage JSON parse/fallback handling is repeated across [authService.ts](src/services/authService.ts), [activityService.ts](src/services/activityService.ts), [journalService.ts](src/services/journalService.ts), [moodService.ts](src/services/moodService.ts), and [chatService.ts](src/services/chatService.ts). Login, signup, and settings repeat page-level try/catch/loading/error patterns. These are candidate consistency improvements, but should be changed only if behavior stays clear.
- Formatting and naming are not fully consistent: mixed quote styles and semicolon conventions appear in the same source tree, as do long inline JSX attributes and densely packed service methods. The package has no Prettier script/config, and `lint` is currently only a TypeScript no-emit command.
- Browser/server diagnostics include `console.error` in [ErrorBoundary.tsx](src/components/ErrorBoundary.tsx) and `console.log` on server startup in [server.ts](server.ts). These are not necessarily dead logging, but need an intentional logging policy rather than a blanket deletion.

## Sample Data

- [authService.ts](src/services/authService.ts) automatically creates a signed-in default user named Riya with `riya@campus.edu` when there is no stored user. The login service also accepts any non-empty email/password of sufficient length and invents a user if it does not find one. This makes demo state look like a real account and the sign-in flow look more complete than it is. “Guest Traveler” and `guest@kai.local` are also synthetic identity details.
- [chatService.ts](src/services/chatService.ts) seeds five highly polished conversations for Riya. They use stylized situations and language (“50 tabs open,” LinkedIn internship comparisons, “autumn leaves,” “quiet hours in the dorm,” “superpower”) and include fixed labels/timestamps that create a false sense of real user history. The default chat greeting also hard-codes Riya; [ChatPage.tsx](src/pages/ChatPage.tsx) has a Riya fallback name.
- [journalService.ts](src/services/journalService.ts) seeds three tidy, lesson-shaped journal entries with retrospective summaries (“Feeling 20% lighter,” “Protecting my rest cycle”) and neat word counts. They read as sample copy, not messy student notes, and are inserted into local storage as if the current user wrote them.
- [moodService.ts](src/services/moodService.ts) inserts a complete 14-day mood log on first load, with a deliberately balanced arc and polished notes. It also reports a minimum five-day streak even when the actual entries do not support one and returns a hard-coded correlation (“45 minutes better”) as a personal insight. Missing chart days are presented as calm values. These turn placeholder analytics into apparently measured wellbeing data.
- [activityService.ts](src/services/activityService.ts) seeds five activities, then forces weekly totals to minimum values (20 minutes, five sessions, and minimum breathing/journal time). Those totals can be shown as the user’s actual activity despite no corresponding actions.
- [DashboardPage.tsx](src/pages/DashboardPage.tsx) contains static “Thursday study stress,” “Last spoke 3 hrs ago,” fixed weekday labels, fixed reflection timestamps, and a hard-coded daily affirmation independent of saved data.
- [LandingPage.tsx](src/pages/LandingPage.tsx) displays a scripted Riya conversation, a fixed five-day streak, “Logged today,” “Active now,” a “40k+” usage statistic, and service/usage promises as live social proof. None is sourced from actual application data.
- [WellnessPage.tsx](src/pages/WellnessPage.tsx) has only six affirmations, each a polished self-help aphorism with a similar reassuring structure, rather than the requested varied 30+. One affirmation is marked as liked by default in component state.
- [SupportPage.tsx](src/pages/SupportPage.tsx) contains four named counselor profiles with invented professional credentials, ratings, availability, locations, and direct `+91` phone numbers, yet labels them verified/certified/licensed and offers call links. These should be clearly labelled demo records and must not present invented contact details as real. The real helplines Tele-MANAS 14416 and iCall 9152987821 are separate entries; preserve and verify those public details if the directory changes.
- The seeded situations are only loosely tied to the requested student context in UP: they mention generic campus life, “dorm,” LinkedIn, and broad exam stress, with few concrete hostel, placement, or local-college details. The prose is also consistently clean and complete instead of varied, casual, or unfinished.

## Docs

- [README.md](README.md) is an untouched-looking AI Studio starter: it says “Run and deploy your AI Studio app,” uses a generic Google AI Studio banner and app URL, and documents `.env.local` even though the repo includes `.env.example`. It does not explain Kai, the student mental-health motivation, current working features, limitations, or future work.
- [.env.example](.env.example) contains AI Studio-specific boilerplate and literal `MY_GEMINI_API_KEY` / `MY_APP_URL` values. It claims AI Studio injects values, describes OAuth callbacks despite no OAuth flow being present, and calls the project an “applet.”
- [package.json](package.json) still identifies the app as `react-example` version `0.0.0`; it has no project description, team author, or license. Its `clean` command uses Unix `rm -rf`, which is not portable to the stated Windows development environment. The `lint` script is a typecheck, not a lint rule set.
- [metadata.json](metadata.json) repeats long generic “warm, empathetic… safe sanctuary” marketing copy and an AI Studio-specific server-side Gemini capability marker. It does not carry meaningful team/project provenance.
- [index.html](index.html) repeats the generic product-description sentence in both description and Open Graph metadata, uses the external orb as favicon, and imports fonts and Material Symbols from third-party Google endpoints without a local visual identity.
- No project README section currently states that the current authentication is local/demo behavior, seeded records are fabricated, chat is sent to a server-side Gemini API, or counselor profiles are illustrative. Those omissions amplify the UI’s unsupported account, privacy, and directory claims.
